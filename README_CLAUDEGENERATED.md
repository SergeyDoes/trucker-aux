# Trucker AUX

Car audio for American Truck Simulator and Euro Truck Simulator 2, in your headphones.
Your music plays from virtual speakers placed in the cab. When you look around in the
game, the speakers stay where they are, as real door speakers would.

> This README was generated by Claude (Anthropic) from the project's code and notes.
> The design notes are in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md), [docs/findings.md](docs/findings.md) and
> the specs in [docs/superpowers/specs/](docs/superpowers/specs/).

## How it works

```
ATS/ETS2 ─► scs-telemetry.dll (RenCloud) ──► shared memory Local\SCSTelemetry
         └► trucker_aux_camera.dll (ours) ─► shared memory Local\TruckerAuxCamera
                                                   │ read at ~100 Hz (koffi)
┌──────────────────── Trucker AUX (Electron) ──────┼──────────────────────────┐
│ main: head pose, data folder, IPC                ▼                          │
│ input device ─(getUserMedia)─► AudioContext → M/S width                     │
│                                 → per speaker: filter → level → HRTF ─────────► headphones
│                                 AudioListener ← head pose, game camera      │
│ presets: key tree, panel, views                                             │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
Player ─► virtual cable                          Game audio ────────────────────► headphones
```

- Any player (browser, Spotify, VLC) plays into a virtual audio cable. Trucker AUX
  records the cable's other end.
- Each virtual speaker is a Web Audio HRTF panner placed in the cab. The listener turns
  and moves with the driver's head, read from the game's telemetry (`head.offset`).
- With `trucker_aux_camera.dll` the listener also turns as the game's camera does, which
  the telemetry leaves out (see Game camera below).
- The game's own sound goes straight to the headphones, outside this chain.

## Features

- **Speakers:**
  - up to 16 per preset;
  - each speaker has a channel (left, right or mono) and a level;
  - mirrored pairs keep the left and right speaker symmetric;
  - speakers are named after their type ("Midbass L", "Tweeter R"), and such a name
    follows a type change.
- **Speaker types,** with Linkwitz–Riley crossovers that sum flat where two types meet:

  | type | band |
  |---|---|
  | full range | no filter |
  | small full range | from 120 Hz |
  | tweeter | from 2.5 kHz |
  | midrange | 500 Hz – 2.5 kHz |
  | midbass | 60 – 500 Hz |
  | subwoofer | up to 120 Hz |

- **Stereo width:** from mono, through as recorded, to extra wide.
- **Editor:**
  - a top view and a side view (SVG) and a 3D overview (three.js);
  - drag speakers and walls, select several with Ctrl+click or a box;
  - copy and paste, also between presets;
  - undo and redo for every change to your presets.
- **Presets on keys:** a preset is set on a key, from narrow to wide:
  - the model, as one of its variants (below);
  - the brand;
  - the game;
  - all vehicles.

  Auto plays the narrowest preset there is for the vehicle in the game.
- **Variants of a model:** a model's cabs are its variants: your presets for it and the
  shared files for it or its hooks. The game reports no cab, only where the fifth wheel sits
  (the hook), so a hook remembers the variant it plays, and so does your own vehicle (by its
  plate), before its hook. A hook new to you remembers the variant picked last (or a file made
  for that hook plays). Edits go to the variant that plays, wherever else it plays. Your vehicle
  can also have a preset of its own, "this vehicle only".
- **The key tree:**
  - keys are grouped by game, brand and model;
  - a model lists its presets as rows under it, each marked "preset" (yours), "default" or
    "shared file", or by its label; another key shows such a mark when set there, else it
    inherits;
  - click a key to play and edit it; editing a key that inherits gives it a copy of its
    own;
  - drag a preset onto a key to add it to that key's variants (moved from its key; Ctrl: a
    copy); what plays there stays. Right-click for more;
  - a model driven for the first time gets its first variant at once, labelled "new": a copy
    of what it would inherit;
  - models of a brand the game names alike (Freightliner Cascadia 2019 and 2024) get the
    year from their id;
  - presets on no key wait under "Unused presets";
  - models are open at first (folded by hand they stay so); the vehicle in the game opens the
    keys down to its own and the row of what plays is scrolled into view, again whenever the
    vehicle, the pick or what plays changes;
  - a key or a preset picked to edit holds until Auto is pressed or you get out of the vehicle:
    the next one plays in Auto.
- **Picking a variant:** the card's "Variant" picks one for the vehicle in the game (its hook,
  or your vehicle's plate, remembers it). The tree shows a model's variant that plays and "+N";
  unfolded, the variants are rows of their own, a model's even when it has one (◆, with their badge and where they play, ▶ on
  the one Auto plays). A click on a row plays it to edit (a default: your copy in its place at
  the first edit); its menu plays it in the vehicle (or makes it the key's current one),
  duplicates, copies or pastes, deletes it (a shared file is hidden from the model; a preset of
  yours goes when no other key uses it) or exports it. Copies join the key's variants, named by
  the key, with the label they had; what plays there stays. Rows drag like keys. Two cabs on one hook (the Kenworth W900's sleeper and studio on 3.6 m)
  are two variants.
- **Controls:** a key, a controller button (a wheel's, a pad's) or a combination of up to three
  steps to the model's next or previous variant, to try it, and another goes back to what fitted
  the cab when you got in; getting out keeps the one tried for that cab. They work while the
  game has the focus. An English Windows voice says what plays: over everything, or from the cab
  speakers like the music (muted with it), at a volume of its own.
- **Labels:** a short coloured tag per preset (up to 32 characters, a longer one than 16
  runs like a ticker), shown beside its keys in the tree. "new" marks a preset the app
  made and goes at its first edit.
- **Shared presets:**
  - **Export…** saves presets ticked in a tree as files, one or a folder of them: a model's
    variant for the hooks that play it. The plates stay private: a vehicle's own preset goes
    out for its hook;
  - **Import…** makes preset files your presets, a file for a model one of its variants, on the
    hooks it is for that play nothing of yours; where one of yours plays it asks which plays;
    a file keeps its name and label;
  - files dropped into `presets/` play without importing, after your own presets.
- **Defaults:**
  - the presets that come with the app sit inside it (`app.asar`; in the source
    `app/presets/default/`), under everything else;
  - a newer build's defaults reach every key you have not set up yourself;
  - see [app/presets/README.md](app/presets/README.md).
- **Real head position:** the driver sits left of the vehicle's centre line by what the
  game reports. The game's seat adjustment moves the listener too.
- **Loudness matching:** a preset with many speakers is not louder than the default two
  doors. The trim is measured: K-weighted pink noise rendered through the engine.
- **Volume:** a slider in Audio, 0–200 %, over everything, in even steps of loudness (a
  50 dB range up to 100 %, then up to +12 dB, as discord/perceptual does); a limiter keeps
  the peaks under 0 dBFS.
- **Like a car radio:**
  - "Mute when" mutes the music while the electrics (the default) or the engine are off;
  - "Pause behavior" sets what happens on pause: always active, always muted, or by
    the vehicle's state.
- **Game camera:** the game's "look into turns" and "look toward the blinker" turn the
  camera, but the telemetry doesn't report them.
  - With `trucker_aux_camera.dll` the sound takes the game camera's own rotation in the
    cab (the status line says "camera: game"), and an outside camera leaves the head at
    rest ("camera: outside").
  - In the game's free camera (developer mode: `g_developer "1"` in the game's
    `config.cfg`, then 0) the sound is heard from where the camera is ("camera: free"):
    fly up to a speaker to hear it up close. "+ Speaker" and "+ Pair" then put the new
    speakers at the camera, a way to place them by looking.
  - Without it, set the same options in the app's "Game camera" fieldset and the turn
    is emulated from the steering and the blinkers. The fieldset shows up only once the
    app has seen the game (a vehicle in the world for 3 s) without the camera.
- **Safe with the game:**
  - the telemetry's shared memory is only opened for reading, never created;
  - `trucker_aux_camera.dll` only reads the game's memory, and every read is guarded:
    a game update that moves things switches it off instead of crashing the game;
  - the app does not read or change the game's files, but Help › Install game plugins copies
    the two plugins into the game folder you pick (looking only for the game's exe there);
  - no sign-ins, no network, but Help › Check for updates when pressed (one request to
    GitHub's releases).
- **Revision check:** the app warns when `scs-telemetry.dll` is a revision it does not
  know (another mod may install its own), as the values it reads could then be wrong.

## Requirements

- Windows 10 or 11.
- American Truck Simulator or Euro Truck Simulator 2.
- Two game plugins, both in the release zip:
  - [RenCloud's scs-sdk-plugin](https://github.com/RenCloud/scs-sdk-plugin),
    `scs-telemetry.dll`;
  - Trucker AUX's `trucker_aux_camera.dll` (optional: without it the game camera is
    emulated). It reads the camera the way the ETS2LA plugin does, so it follows game
    versions (built for 1.61).
- A virtual audio cable. Any of these works:
  - [VB-Audio Virtual Cable](https://vb-audio.com/Cable/), free;
  - [Virtual Audio Cable](https://vac.muzychenko.net/en/), paid;
  - Steam Streaming Speakers and Microphone. Steam installs them for Remote Play.
- Plain stereo headphones. Virtual surround (DTS Headphone:X, Windows Sonic, Dolby
  Atmos) would virtualize the binaural output a second time.

## Setup

1. Download the latest release zip and extract it.
2. Copy `plugin\scs-telemetry.dll` and `plugin\trucker_aux_camera.dll` to
   `<game>\bin\win_x64\plugins\` (create `plugins` if needed), or run the app and use
   Help › Install game plugins: pick the game's folder, and a plugin there of another build
   is renamed `<file>.backup-<time>` first. On start the game asks about "advanced SDK
   features": accept.
3. Set up the audio:
   - route your player to the cable's input (in Windows' volume mixer, per app);
   - use one sample rate everywhere: both sides of the cable and the headphones. Any
     mismatch causes dropouts;
   - VB-Cable: in its control panel set Max Latency to 7168 and Internal SR to that rate.
4. Start `Trucker AUX.exe`:
   - set Input to the cable's output;
   - set Output to your headphones;
   - if `trucker_aux_camera.dll` is not used, set "Game camera" as in the game's
     Accessibility options. The fieldset appears once you drive without it.

To run from source instead:

```bash
git clone --recursive <this repository>
cd app
npm install
npm start
```

Build the plugins in Visual Studio 2022:
- RenCloud's: open `third_party/scs-sdk-plugin/scs-telemetry/vs2012/scs-telemetry.vcxproj`
  and build Release x64. The `.sln` has no x64 configuration, so build the project;
- ours: run `native/camera-plugin/build.cmd`; the DLL lands in `native/camera-plugin/out/`.

## Using it

- **The window:** from left to right:
  - the **key tree** with "Unused presets" below it;
  - the **panel**: audio, camera (where the head comes from), variant control bindings, the preset and
    its card, speakers;
  - the **top and side views**;
  - the **3D overview**, with the **bounds** under it.
  - The panel's parts fold at a click on their title, and stay so.
- **Auto** follows the vehicle in the game. Picking a key or a preset stops that; the
  Auto button above the tree or "Back to Auto" on the card brings it back.
- **The card** under the preset list:
  - shows the vehicle, what plays and the keys of its vehicle ("Applies to");
  - picking a key there moves the preset up or down, and the app asks first when that
    key is taken.
- **Variant Control Bindings:** click Switch Next, Switch Prev. or Switch Auto, press a key, a
  controller button or up to three together, and let go; Escape cancels, × clears. Next and
  Prev. play the model's variants as a pick in the list does; Auto goes back to the one that
  fitted when you got in, and getting out (another vehicle, or the game closed) keeps the one
  tried for that cab. At first they are `.`, `,` and `/`, keys the games' layout leaves free. The
  game sees these keys and buttons too, so pick ones it does not use.
- **Camera › Mode:** *Raw Camera - Recommended*, the game's camera through
  `trucker_aux_camera.dll` as it is (the telemetry when it does not read it); *Raw Telemetry -
  VR Recommended*, the telemetry's head as it is, a headset's pose, nothing added; *Adjusted
  Telemetry*, for when Raw Camera misbehaves: the telemetry with the look into turns and toward
  the blinker set below, as in the game's Accessibility options. The status line says *camera:
  raw telemetry* or *camera: adjusted telemetry* for those.
- **Camera › Lock:** yaw, pitch and roll can be locked per mode: a locked axis of the head stays
  straight in the cab and the sound does not follow it. At first the roll is locked for Raw
  Camera and Adjusted Telemetry (the game's camera leans against the cab as it sways), nothing for
  Raw Telemetry.
  The free camera's turns are its own.
- **Audio › Control voice:** says which variant plays when you switch: Off; System, over
  everything; AUX, from the cab speakers, muted with the music. Voice volume is its own; Test
  says what plays now.
- **Devices:** a new setup records from a virtual cable, told by the source in brackets after a
  device's name, which both sides of a cable share and Windows does not translate: VB-Audio
  Virtual Cable (and Cable A/B, Hi-Fi), Virtual Audio Cable, Steam Streaming Speakers, in that
  order; never a microphone. It plays to the default output, unless that is a virtual device.
  Under Input the panel names the cable's other side, where the player is to send the music.
- **Coordinates:**
  - X goes right from the vehicle's centre line;
  - Y goes up and Z goes back, from the driver's default head;
  - the "0" in the views is that point.
- **Bounds** only frame the views for placing speakers; they don't change the sound.
- **Shortcuts,** outside text fields:
  - arrows nudge the selected speakers, Shift+arrows by 10 cm;
  - Delete removes them, Esc clears the selection;
  - Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Ctrl+D select all, copy, paste and duplicate;
  - after a click in the tree, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Ctrl+D copy the preset picked there, paste a
    copy onto the key picked, and duplicate it;
  - Ctrl+Z undoes; Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes;
  - F2 renames an unused preset.
- **Data** is kept next to the app, not in `%APPDATA%`. From source these folders are
  in `app/`:
  - `data/layouts.json`: your presets and keys;
  - `data/settings.json`: devices and options;
  - `data/profile/`: Chromium's profile;
  - `data/crash.log` and `data/crashes/`: a line and a dump for each crash, kept here, never sent;
  - `presets/`: preset files from others. The app reads them and never changes them;
  - the presets that come with the app are inside it: a newer version unpacked over the old
    folder replaces them whole and keeps `data/` and `presets/`. A `presets/default/` left by a
    version before 0.3.0 is not read.
- **Menus:** File › Open data folder; Edit › Undo and Redo (the window's Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y,
  so a text field still undoes its own text); View › Reset panel layout (the panels unfolded,
  the tree's width as at first); Help › Open crash log (`data/crash.log`, and `data/crashes/`
  when it holds a dump).
- **File › Import / Export:**
  - Export › All data writes `data/` (presets and settings) and your `presets/` files into a
    new folder laid out like the app's;
  - Import › Data from older version, From data (only presets) and From data (only settings)
    bring them from such a folder or another version's. The current files are copied into
    `data/backup-<time>/` first, the defaults of an older version are never brought, and the
    window reloads with what came;
  - Export › All presets saves every preset of yours as files without the export window;
    Export › Presets opens it, Import › Preset files is the Presets panel's import.

## Development

```bash
cd app
npm test             # unit tests (node --test)
npm run test:engine  # renders the audio graph offline and checks it in dB
npm run dist         # Windows build: out/Trucker AUX-<version>-win-x64.zip
```

- `npm run dist` needs both built plugins in `app/plugin/`, which are git-ignored.
- A build hides the debug tools: the test-file source and DevTools. Start it with
  `--dev-tools` to have them.
- Updating the defaults: `node scripts/export-store.mjs <data folder> <out folder>` exports the
  tree of the presets played there; put its files in place of `app/presets/default/`, then
  build. A default keeps its file name (as it is, or as your copy of it), and other presets get
  names no default had, so a hook remembering a default finds the same cab after an update.
- `tools/fake_shm.py` fakes the telemetry for work without the game: a game, a truck, a
  fifth-wheel position, a plate, a blinker and, with `--camera cab|outside|free`, the
  camera plugin's block. Run it only while the game is closed.
- `tools/camera_probe.py` records the game camera (from the ETS2LA plugin) beside the
  telemetry; `tools/camera_block.py` prints which camera `trucker_aux_camera.dll` reads;
  `tools/shm_probe.py` records the telemetry for a drive.
- `tools/cable_tone.py` and `tools/cable_check.py` check the virtual cable for dropouts.

```
app/                        Electron app
  src/main/                 window, IPC, data folder, shared-memory readers, preset files
  src/shared/               pure logic: pose, game camera, layouts, presets and keys, DSP
  src/renderer/             audio engine, key tree, panel, views, 3D overview, dialogs
  test/                     unit tests
  scripts/                  engine check, build checks
  presets/                  preset files; default/ ships with the app
native/camera-plugin        trucker_aux_camera.dll (C++, Visual Studio 2022)
third_party/scs-sdk-plugin  RenCloud's telemetry plugin (submodule)
tools/                      Python helpers
docs/                       findings from the game, specs and plans
```

## Limitations

- Windows only.
- The game doesn't report the cab or the cab's size, so speakers and bounds are set by
  hand. Cabs are told apart by the fifth-wheel position, which some cabs share: they are
  variants of their model, picked by hand (or with the controls).
- Without `trucker_aux_camera.dll` an outside camera can't be told from the cab one: the
  emulated turn look then turns the sound on an outside camera too.
- `trucker_aux_camera.dll` depends on the game version: after an update it may switch
  itself off until it is updated, and the emulation takes over.
- The first run knows VB-Audio's cables, Virtual Audio Cable and Steam's streaming speakers by
  their source; with another cable, pick its output by hand.
